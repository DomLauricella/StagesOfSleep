The Stages of Sleep

Sleep is not one steady state. Your brain and body move through distinct stages, each with its own rhythm. Here is what happens through the night, with a closer look at REM sleep.

01 / THE STAGES

Getting to know your sleep.

Non-REM sleep has three stages: N1, N2, and N3. REM is a separate phase. These stages repeat through the night, rather than happening just once. [1]

N1

NON-REM

Drifting off

The brief transition from being awake to being asleep. Breathing slows and muscles begin to relax, sometimes with a small twitch.

The start of sleep

N2

NON-REM

Settling in

Body temperature falls and eye movements stop. Brain activity slows, with short bursts of electrical activity called sleep spindles.

A lighter stage of sleep

N3

NON-REM

Deep sleep

Slow brain waves mark this stage. It is harder to wake up, and the body supports tissue growth and repair.

More common early in the night

REM

RAPID EYE MOVEMENT

An active brain

Brain activity becomes more like waking activity. The eyes move rapidly, and most dreaming occurs during this stage.

02 / A CLOSER LOOK

REM sleep.
Still body,
active mind.

REM stands for rapid eye movement. It is named for the quick movements of the eyes beneath closed eyelids.

About 90 minutesafter falling asleep, the first REM period usually begins. [2]
01

The brain becomes more active

Brain-wave patterns resemble those seen while awake. Breathing becomes faster and less regular, and heart rate rises toward waking levels. [2]

02

Most muscles stay quiet

The muscles in your arms and legs temporarily lose most of their tone. This normal state, called muscle atonia, helps keep you from acting out dreams. [2]

03

Dreaming is common

Most dreaming happens during REM, but dreams can occur in non-REM sleep too. Dreaming does not belong to just one stage. [2]

04

Memories are being processed

Sleep helps strengthen and connect memories. REM may help link related information, while non-REM sleep also contributes. Researchers are still working out how these processes fit together. [4]

03 / COMMON QUESTIONS

A few things to clear up.

Is REM the same as deep sleep?

No. Deep sleep refers to N3, a non-REM stage marked by slow brain waves. REM has a different brain-activity pattern and is its own phase. [1]

Why is there more REM toward morning?

The balance of sleep stages changes across the night. Deep sleep is more prominent early on, while later cycles generally contain longer REM periods. Your sleep pattern is not identical in every cycle. [3]

Does every cycle last exactly 90 minutes?

No. Cycle length varies. The NHLBI describes cycles of about 80 to 100 minutes, usually repeated four to six times a night. The diagram above is an illustration, not a timetable. [1]
